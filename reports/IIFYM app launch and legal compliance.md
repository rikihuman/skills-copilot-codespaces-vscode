# Ship the macro coach without courting lawsuits

Both app stores will accept this app as long as it stays a **general-wellness tool**: in practice that means no disease claims and a "No" on Apple's medical-device declaration. For a small South African subscription health app, the likeliest legal problems are not a user suing over a calorie target. They are **billing and cancellation complaints, health data leaking through ad or analytics SDKs, data breaches and unsubstantiated weight-loss marketing**. Those areas account for the large precedents: Noom's **$62M** auto-renewal settlement, the **2025 jury verdict against Meta** over Flo Health period data, the FTC's GoodRx and BetterHelp penalties, and the National Consumer Tribunal's ruling against Vodacom's cancellation penalties. Personal-injury claims are rarer but severe, and the NEDA "Tessa" chatbot is the template. It gave standard 500–1,000 kcal deficit advice to an eating-disorder population. The launch requirements change the earlier app plan in six ways. You need a **(Pty) Ltd and organization developer accounts** (D-U-N-S lead time of up to ~6 weeks). The app should be **18+ only**. The **human-coach tier stays on in-app purchase** on both stores, because asynchronous coaching is not the "real-time 1:1" exemption. Pricing must absorb **15% VAT plus a ~15% store fee**, so a R149/mo plan nets about **R110**. The AI coach needs **provider-named consent, a report button and an "AI" label**. **Cycle tracking stays deferred** until its own consent and data pipeline exist. In South Africa the gating items are POPIA explicit consent for health data, Information Officer registration, CPA s49 risk notices, and an attorney opinion on sending health data to offshore LLM APIs (s72/s57). Much of what follows comes from search snippets, third-party summaries or the researchers' own knowledge of statute text, not from fetched primary pages. Those items are marked **[U]**. **This report is technical research, not legal advice.** Have an SA attorney review it before launch.

*Marker key: **[U]** = unverified. It comes from a search snippet, a secondary source or training knowledge, and must be checked against the primary source. Citations point to what the research notes actually relied on.*

The earlier idea of seeding retailer barcodes needs a licensing source, not scraping.

### What the store fee and VAT do to the price table

The arithmetic below assumes the stores calculate commission on the VAT-exclusive price in territories where they remit VAT. That is standard store practice but **[U]**. Confirm it in App Store Connect and the Play payments profile, and confirm Google's SA VAT handling in particular, which the research could not establish ([Google Play tax info](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/2850368)).

| Plan (consumer price incl. VAT) | Ex-VAT | Proceeds at 15% fee | Proceeds at 10% (Apple SBP, subscriber year 2+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pro R149/mo | R129.57 | **R110.13** | R116.61 |
| Pro R999/yr | R868.70 | **R738.39** | R781.83 |
| Coach R599/mo | R520.87 | **R442.74** | R468.78 |

Roughly **26% of the sticker price never reaches you**, before the coach's share and LLM costs. Web billing through PayFast or Paystack ([PayFast](https://payfast.io/blog/launching-payfast-recurring-billing)) avoids the store fee and, below the VAT threshold, the VAT too. But outside the US storefront, the app cannot link to or advertise a web checkout without an entitlement ([Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Web billing also brings ECTA s43/s44 duties and CPA cancellation duties back onto you, where the stores currently carry them. Use IAP for launch, and treat web-sold coaching as a later, separate funnel with no links from inside the app.

## South Africa: POPIA consent and CPA risk notices carry the weight

Weight, body measurements, screening answers and cycle data are almost certainly **"health information"**, which POPIA classes as special personal information. For a consumer app, the only practical s27 ground is **express, specific, informed consent**, and the responsible party bears the burden of proving it ([Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2026/04/health-data-under-the-microscope-new-popia-regulations); [POPIA](https://www.gov.za/documents/protection-personal-information-act) [U on section text]). The 2026 **Health Information Regulations** (GG 54268, 6 Mar 2026) target insurers, medical schemes, pension funds and employers. Bowmans says they largely reflect the existing framework ([Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/south-africa-popia-health-information-regulations-cross-the-finish-line/)). Whether a consumer nutrition app falls outside them is an attorney question. On the CPA side, a paid subscription is a "service". The **s49 risk notice** (conspicuous, plain-language, actively acknowledged) is the clause that most protects you, because **s51 voids exclusions of gross negligence** and **Regulation 44 presumes death and injury exclusions unfair** ([MJK Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/compliance/consumer-protection-act/unfair-contract-terms); [Reg 44](https://www.acts.co.za/consumer-protection/r293_44__list_of_contract_terms_which_are_presumed)). The SCA in *UPS SCS v Van Wyk* struck down a limitation clause that had not been drawn to the consumer's attention.

| 7 | **Coach scope / title misuse** (HPCSA offence, negligent coaching) | Low–medium (scales with the marketplace) | Medium | HPCSA protected titles. No SA case found |
| 8 | **Database IP** (ODbL non-compliance, scraping) | Low–medium | Low–medium | OFF actively sends non-compliance notices ([OFF wiki](https://wiki.openfoodfacts.org/ODBL_non-compliance/Example_email)) |
| 9 | **Medical-device reclassification** (SAHPRA/FDA/MDR) | Low if claims stay wellness | High | TGA/FDA: disease claims make an app a device, and disclaimers don't override that ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/consumer-information-and-resources/software-based-medical-devices-consumers)) |
| 10 | **Accessibility suits** | Low for an SA micro-company | Low–medium | **5,114** US ADA digital suits in 2025, 45% against repeat targets ([UsableNet](https://info.usablenet.com/2025-year-end-digital-accessibility-lawsuit-report-plain-text)) |
| 11 | **AI-specific regulation** (EU AI Act, SB 243) | Low with disclosure | Medium | FTC 6(b) orders to seven chatbot firms, Sep 2025 ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)) |

The pattern matters for prioritisation. The two highest-likelihood risks are **engineering choices made once, in the billing flow and in the dependency list**, and neither costs much to get right. The injury risk is the reverse. It needs continuous controls (screening, floors, AI guardrails, model-change testing), and its best legal defence is **evidence that those controls existed**: consent logs, dietitian sign-off and algorithm versions stored with each check-in.

## Mitigations wire into specific features, not a terms page

Disclaimers do not cure a product that actively recommends something unsafe. Courts and regulators look at design. The earlier plan's engine already has floors and a deterministic-only rule for targets. The table below finishes the job, feature by feature.

| Feature | Mitigation | Satisfies |
|---|---|---|
| **Onboarding screening** | A DOB gate (18+ hard stop). PAR-Q-style questions: current or past eating disorder (SCOFF-style), pregnancy or breastfeeding, insulin or sulfonylureas, CKD, GLP-1 drugs, heart conditions. **A positive answer routes to maintenance or a non-numeric mode and blocks deficits**; a warning alone is not enough. Persist answers plus the notice version | Injury defence. CPA s49. Apple 1.4.1 |
| **Risk acknowledgement** | A separate screen with plain-language risks ("estimates for healthy adults; not suitable if…") and an unticked checkbox, shown before the first target. Repeat it in context when a deficit is generated | CPA s49 assent, *Van Wyk* |
| **Health-data consents** | Four independent, versioned toggles: (1) health and body data, (2) cycle data (only when shipped), (3) AI coach processing, naming the provider and the data sent, (4) marketing (opt-in). The app must work if (2)–(4) are declined, and paid features cannot depend on optional consents | POPIA s11/s27/s69. GDPR Art. 9. Apple 5.1.1(ii)/(iv), 5.1.2(i) |
| **Adaptive engine** | Floor = max(1,200 kcal ♀ / 1,500 kcal ♂, BMR × 1.1). Loss ≤1% BW/week. No deficit at BMI <18.5. Protein warning if the CKD flag is set. There is a real tension between **logging AI transcripts for safety evidence and POPIA data minimisation**. Resolve it with a stated retention period (for example 90 days, an inference) disclosed in the privacy notice. Keep flagged conversations longer under legal hold.

### Insurance and operational controls round out the defence

Buy four layers. **Tech E&O/professional indemnity** must explicitly cover algorithmic and AI-generated advice, with a bodily-injury carve-back. **Cyber liability** should cover breach response, notification, regulatory defence and, where insurable, fines. Add **general and product liability**, and **require coaches to carry their own PI**. Put five coverage questions to the broker. Many SA policies **exclude US and Canadian jurisdiction** by default, so ask for worldwide cover. Some insurers are adding AI exclusions. Cyber policies may exclude wiretap or privacy-statute class actions (CIPA-type). Ask about regulatory defence costs for POPIA, GDPR and FTC matters. And ask about media/IP cover for database disputes. No premium data was found, so get a quote. Insurers and courts both value the same records: dietitian review of the engine, an incident-response plan, AI red-team logs, consent records, vendor DPAs and change management for model updates. These broker asks are research inference, not sourced policy wording. For a sense of breach scale, the MyFitnessPal incident needed forced resets and outside forensics ([Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/70814/data-breach/under-armour-hack.html)).

## Fourteen questions to put to an SA attorney

1. Does sending health or cycle data (even pseudonymised) to a US LLM API, or to any offshore Azure region, need **s57 prior authorisation**, or do s72(1)(a) contractual safeguards plus s72(1)(b) consent suffice?
2. Do the **2026 Health Information Regulations** (GG 54268) apply to a consumer nutrition and coaching app?
3. Where is the line between lawful unregistered **"nutrition/macro coaching"** and the reserved scope in the 2024 Dietitians scope regulations? Is "nutrition coach" a safe title?
4. For **store-billed subscriptions**, is the developer or Apple/Google the "supplier" for CPA s14 and ECTA s43/s44 purposes?
5. Is an annual auto-renewing plan a **CPA fixed-term agreement** that triggers the 80–40 business-day notice and s14 penalty limits? What penalty, if any, is reasonable?
6. Does the ECTA s42(2) exclusion ("services began with the consumer's consent") remove the 7-day cooling-off for immediately used subscriptions? What checkout acknowledgement does it need?
7. Please review the wording and UX of the **s49 risk notice** and an s51/Reg 44-compliant liability cap.
8. Is self-declared DOB enough **18+ gating** under POPIA s34–35, or is age assurance needed?
9. Is the human coach an **operator or a separate responsible party** under POPIA? How should coach-marketplace liability be allocated?
10. VAT: how should app-store proceeds and direct web sales be treated under the R2.3M threshold, and which tax invoices do the stores issue?
11. If the app launches **SA-only** on the stores, does any EU/UK representative or GDPR exposure remain (for example, SA users travelling, or a web sign-up)?
12. What is the licensing route for **SAFOODS data and retailer barcode data**? Is there an SA database-right or copyright risk in compilations?
13. What consent form should be used for **testimonials and before/after images** (POPIA health information plus image rights), and is the ARB code binding on a non-member?
14. Which **insurance** wording is needed for worldwide jurisdiction and AI output, and is an indemnity from the LLM vendor obtainable?

## Conclusion

The compliance work reorders the roadmap more than it adds to it. The earlier plan treated safety as an ethical differentiator: the ED-safe mode, floors, and an LLM that cannot touch targets. The legal record shows those same features are the **liability defence**, but only if they leave evidence: versioned consents, stored screening answers, engine versions recorded with each check-in, and a dietitian's signature. The costliest mistakes are cheap to avoid and easy to make by accident. One marketing SDK on a weigh-in screen or one obstructed cancel button puts a micro-app in the same legal category as Flo or Noom.

The practical strategy is **SA-first, IAP-only, 18+, no third-party SDKs on health data**. Defer global storefronts until the attorney has answered the s57 transfer question and EU/UK representatives are in place. That trades some early reach for a launch that carries mostly South African, mostly contract-shaped risk. It also gives the business time to see whether ~74% net-of-fees proceeds still support the R399–599 coach tier once coach payouts and LLM costs are counted.
