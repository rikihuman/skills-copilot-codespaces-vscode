# Build a calm, legible macro coach interface

The best fit for this app is a **deep "fynbos ink-teal" brand (`#134E4A` light / `#99F6E4` dark)** on neutral surfaces. Macros get four lightness-separated hues: **indigo protein, orange carbs, magenta fat and emerald fibre**. Calories are shown in **neutral ink**, and no red or amber is used anywhere in the nutrition domain. The macro set stays clearly distinct under deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia (worst pairwise ΔE ≈ 22 light, 24 dark). Moving the brand to a darker ink-teal and fibre to emerald resolves the clash in the notes, where fibre was the same teal as the brand. The decision rests on the design rather than on colour psychology. No industry convention exists for macro colours ([Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/7167); [MFP blog](https://blog.myfitnesspal.com/why-were-changing-the-myfitnesspal-color-palette-again/)). Red "over budget" states are tied to shame in first-person and forum accounts ([The Mighty](https://themighty.com/2020/03/my-eating-disorder-started-with-app/); [Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/1350/new-cronometer-update-isnt-great-if-youre-recovering-from-an-eating-disorder)). The leading adaptive coach shows met or over targets as plain black or white bars ([MacroFactor help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/22-get-to-know-your-dashboard)). The interface follows the same logic. It has **four tabs (Today, Progress, Coach, Learn) plus a persistent "+ Log" control**, horizontal macro bars instead of nested rings, and a trend-weight chart with faint dots under a bold line. Every live number uses tabular figures. A Calm mode removes numbers everywhere. The client stack is **React Native with Expo (Expo Router, Reanimated, React Native Skia charts) over the existing .NET 10 backend**, chosen by the developer for UI headroom. The cost is porting the on-device part of the adaptive engine to TypeScript and keeping it in lockstep with the C# server engine through shared test vectors. Widgets still need thin native Swift/Kotlin extensions, and Skia charts need hand-built accessibility summaries. None of the palettes has been tested with users. Every contrast and colour-vision-deficiency (CVD) figure here is a calculation, not an observation, and the two-week test plan at the end is designed to close that gap.

## One palette: ink-teal brand, four CVD-safe macro hues, neutral calories

The research compared three candidate directions and kept the macro set that survived simulation of all three dichromacies. Machado 2009 matrices were applied at full severity, and distances were measured as CIE76 ΔE, where below 10 is hard to tell apart and 20 or more is clearly distinct. The tempting alternatives failed badly. A "blue/amber/purple/green" set collapsed protein into fat under deuteranopia (ΔE 5.3). A warm-earth set made carbs and fibre almost identical under protanopia (ΔE 0.6). A lime fibre tested for this report failed the same way against orange carbs (ΔE 0.6). The lesson is that **lightness separation matters more than hue**. Contrast is computed with the WCAG relative-luminance formula ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)) against the page background, with the value on cards in brackets where it matters.

Teal also places the brand outside the orange space held by Noom, Cronometer and Lose It! and the blue space held by MyFitnessPal.

### The fibre-versus-brand clash is resolved by moving the brand, not by adding a pattern

In the notes' "Fynbos Teal" candidate, `#0F766E` was both the brand and the fibre colour. That makes a fibre bar look like a button or a selected state, and it makes the brand read as "good nutrient". Two fixes were tested. Changing fibre to lime or olive fails, because it collapses into orange carbs for protanopes (ΔE 0.6 and 9.3). Changing fibre to cyan fails against the teal brand for tritanopes (ΔE 4.1). The fix that survives is to **darken the brand to ink-teal `#134E4A` and shift fibre to emerald `#047857`**. Brand and fibre then sit 26.6 ΔE apart for normal vision and at least 17.3 apart under every CVD simulation in light mode. In dark mode the figures are 31.9 and 17.0 (`#99F6E4` vs `#34D399`). A usage rule backs up the colour change: **brand tokens never appear inside data marks, and macro tokens never style controls.** The darker brand also gives white button text 9.48:1 instead of 5.47:1, which helps outdoors in high-UV South African light. The cost is a slightly more sombre identity. `brand.container` mint and illustration warmth make up for that.

## Five colour rules keep nutrition data morally neutral

The evidence on hue and appetite is weak and points the opposite way from the folklore. In small samples, red *reduced* intake and acted as a "stop" signal ([Genschow et al.](https://edoc-vmtest.ub.unibas.ch/21254/6/red_reduces_food_and_drink_intake_revision-1.pdf)). That is exactly why red should not sit on food. The harm evidence is qualitative but consistent. In one clinical sample, **73% of eating-disorder patients who used MyFitnessPal said it contributed to their disorder** ([Levinson et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28843591/)). A user describes the budget bar turning red and feeling "like a total failure" ([The Mighty](https://themighty.com/2020/03/my-eating-disorder-started-with-app/)). Cronometer users found red fat totals read as warnings ([Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/18623)). MacroFactor markets the opposite stance: "You'll never see warnings, red numbers, or shaming" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/bg/app/id1553503471)). Five rules follow from this.

First, **no red or amber in the nutrition domain**. Food, macros, calories, weight and adherence never use `status.error` or `status.caution`. Those tokens exist only for system states, and a lint rule in the token pipeline should block them from any `nutrition.*` or `chart.*` component. Second, **met and over states go neutral, not alarming**. A bar fills in its macro hue up to target. Once the target is met it becomes `text.primary` ink, as MacroFactor does with black or white bars and a grey target marker. Any overshoot past the tolerance band is drawn as an ink hatch with a quiet "+120 kcal" label. Overage text is off by default, as in MacroFactor's opt-in negative numbers ([MacroFactor help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/42-switch-between-consumed-and-remaining-views?_rsc=1acxs)). Third, **targets are bands, not lines**. Calories get a ±5–10% tinted zone (20–30% tint of the series colour), and protein is shown as a floor ("≥ 150 g"). The band width is a product decision with no user evidence behind it yet. Fourth, **weight direction is never coloured**. Gain and loss use the same ink trend line, phase bands use neutral tints with text labels, and loss gets no celebratory green. Fifth, **colour is never the only cue**, as WCAG SC 1.4.1 requires ([W3C WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). Every macro carries a P/C/F/Fi letter and a fixed order (protein first). Status changes add text or icons, and hatching marks overage. Two further constraints apply over time. The mapping must **never change after launch**, because Cronometer and MFP both drew backlash for changing theirs ([Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/354/graph-color-coding-inconsistency)). Noom-style green/yellow/orange food grading is excluded outright ([Noom support](https://www.noom.com/support/faqs/using-the-app/logging-and-tracking/food-and-water/2025/10/how-nooms-food-color-system-works/)).

## Tabular numerals and three chart specs carry the data

### Typography and number display

Use **system fonts for chrome and body text** so that Dynamic Type, localisation and Liquid Glass legibility tuning come free: SF Pro on iOS, with SF Pro Rounded for hero numerals in the Apple Health style, and Roboto Flex on Android ([Axiom typography reference](https://charleswiltgen.github.io/Axiom/reference/typography-ref)). **Turn on tabular figures for every number that changes or is compared**, so that calorie counters do not jitter and columns line up ([Preuss Type: tnum](https://preusstype.com/techdata/otf_tnum.php); [Apple monospacedDigit()](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/monospaceddigit().md)). For a cross-platform brand numeral face, use **Inter v4 from rsms/inter**, not the Google Fonts build, which strips OpenType features including `tnum` ([Inter wiki](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Inter_(typeface))). Use its ss01/ss02 alternates to separate 1/l/7 and 0/O, as Blender does ([Blender PR #112795](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/112795)). Manrope is an OFL fallback. SF fonts cannot ship on Android under Apple's licence (my reading of the licence, not verified here).

The numeral hierarchy has three levels. Each screen has **one hero number**: calories remaining or consumed on Today, and trend weight on Progress. It is set at 48–64 pt semibold and tabular, and it scales with Dynamic Type. Macro grams are title-size, with the unit lighter and smaller ("142 g"). Deltas and targets are caption-size, signed and arrowed, and never colour-only. SA locale details are the **comma decimal separator** ("81,4 kg") and a **kJ/kcal toggle**, because SA labels print kilojoules. Numeric keypads must accept the comma. At the largest accessibility sizes, macro bars stack vertically and every chart offers "View as table". In React Native, set `fontVariant: ['tabular-nums']` on every numeric `Text` style; this maps to the platform's tabular-figure feature on iOS and Android (from React Native docs knowledge, verify with the bundled Inter build on both platforms).

Android has no audio-graph equivalent, so semantics summaries and the table view are the parity path.

## Four tabs and a persistent Log button organise eight screens

The navigation has **four single-word tabs: Today · Progress · Coach · Learn**. **"+ Log"** is a persistent action rather than a tab, and settings, Calm mode and data export live behind a profile avatar on Today. On iOS 26, "+ Log" is the tab-bar **bottom accessory** (`tabViewBottomAccessory`). It stays reachable when the bar minimises on scroll ([Apple HIG: Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars.md); [WWDC25 284](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/284/)). On Android it is a FAB above the **M3 Expressive flexible navigation bar**, with a navigation rail on tablets. M3 Expressive deprecates the navigation drawer ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/05/14/material-3-expressive-navigation/)). The diary folds into Today through a date scroller. Carbon's precedent puts check-ins on Coach ([Carbon Help](https://help.joincarbon.com/en/articles/6004812-weekly-check-in-in-carbon-how-it-works-and-what-to-expect)). No tab-structure usability study exists for nutrition apps, so validate the structure with tree tests.

### Platform conventions and accessibility

**Liquid Glass belongs on navigation chrome only.** Infinum's beta testing found some iOS 26 screens "well below WCAG minimum contrast", and Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast were "not enough" ([Infinum](https://infinum.com/blog/apples-ios-26-liquid-glass-sleek-shiny-and-questionably-accessible/)). Apple then added a Tinted option in 26.1 ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/companies/apple-yields-tinted-control-in-ios-261-beta-4-tones-down-liquid-glass-after-backlash-1.500315176)). All macro data therefore sits on opaque `bg.surface` cards, and brand colour stays out of the glass bar and lives inside content. On Android, generate M3 tonal roles from the `#134E4A` seed with Material Theme Builder, then override the macro and status roles with the fixed tokens above so that dynamic colour cannot recolour data ([Material Theme Builder](https://www.Github.com/material-foundation/material-theme-builder)). M3 Expressive's "4× faster to spot" claim comes from Google's own research ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/get-started/benefits)). Haptics should be light: a selection tick on steppers, a light impact on log, and a success notification on accepting a check-in. There is **no error haptic for going over target** and no celebration for weight loss, and Reduce Motion is respected ([Apple HIG: Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics.md)). Icons are SF Symbols and Material Symbols, outline style, with food icons never morally coloured. Accessibility checklist: tap targets of at least 44 pt / 48 dp; every bar and ring is an accessibility element with a full sentence label; charts have descriptors and table views; Dynamic Type has been tested at AX5; and widgets and notifications respect Calm mode.

## React Native (Expo) client over the .NET backend

**Decision (developer's choice, 10 Nov 2026): React Native with Expo**, replacing the earlier MAUI recommendation. The reason is UI headroom: Reanimated, Gesture Handler and React Native Skia give finer control over custom bars, the trend chart and logging micro-interactions than MAUI, and the ecosystem (RevenueCat, HealthKit/Health Connect bindings, camera/barcode, EAS over-the-air updates) is larger. The backend stays .NET 10. Package names below are from the developer's and assistant's own knowledge, not this research round; check current versions and maintenance status before committing [U].

| Concern | Choice | Notes / risks |
|---|---|---|
| App shell | **Expo** (managed workflow + dev client), **Expo Router**, TypeScript strict | Expo Router **native tabs** give the real iOS 26 tab bar (Liquid Glass, minimise-on-scroll); confirm bottom-accessory support for "+ Log", else use a floating button [U] |
| Glass / chrome | `expo-glass-effect` (iOS 26) on navigation chrome only | Data stays on opaque cards, as above |

**Engine split.** Two options were weighed. (a) **Recommended:** TypeScript engine on-device for everything offline (remaining macros, trend, previews, guardrails), with the C# engine on the server running the authoritative weekly check-in in Azure Functions. Shared golden vectors stop drift. (b) Server-only engine: simpler, but check-ins need a connection and the phone still needs basic remaining-macro maths. Keep guardrails (calorie floors, minimum protein, max loss rate) in **both** engines, so neither path can emit an unsafe target.

**What React Native costs versus MAUI:** a second language (TypeScript) and the engine port; chart accessibility built by hand, the same as MAUI with Skia; Android Material fidelity built from tokens. **What it buys:** stronger animation and custom UI, a larger hiring pool, OTA updates, and first-party Expo support for iOS 26 chrome. Uno/Avalonia stay ruled out for the screen-reader reason above; native SwiftUI + Compose remains the escape hatch for any screen that feels second-class.

### Design-token pipeline

Define all colour, type, spacing and radius tokens as **Figma Variables with Light and Dark modes**. Export them to **W3C DTCG JSON**, stable 1.0 since Oct 2025 according to secondary sources, using Tokens Studio. Figma's native export does not match DTCG exactly, and text styles need separate handling ([Codercops](https://blog.codercops.com/blog/design-tokens-2026-w3c-format-guide); [Tokens Studio](https://docs.tokens.studio/figma/export/styles-variable-references); [DEV](https://dev.to/slafleche/even-figma-isnt-sure-about-its-own-design-tokens-4mko)). **Style Dictionary** then builds three outputs: a typed **TypeScript theme** (`tokens.ts` with light/dark objects, consumed through a `useTheme()` hook driven by `useColorScheme()`) that both components and Victory Native charts read, a Swift colour set for WidgetKit, and Kotlin colours for Glance. Widgets and app then share one source. Name tokens semantically (`macro.protein`, `chart.trend`, `chart.scaleDot`, `target.band`, `status.error`). Add a CI step that recomputes every contrast pair and the CVD ΔE matrix on each token change, and that fails the build if a `status.error` or `status.caution` reference appears in a nutrition or chart component.

## What remains unverified, and a two-week test plan

Several inputs rest on snippets or the researchers' own knowledge rather than on fetched primary pages. The **MFP macro hexes** (`#EC5E8A`/`#F0A020`/`#A05EC5`) come from a third-party site, and Cronometer's brand hex comes from an aggregator. **MacroFactor's per-macro hues are unknown**. Apple HIG and Material pages on colour and Liquid Glass custom tinting were seen only through summaries. The SF-on-Android licence restriction is unconfirmed. The React Native package choices (Expo Router native tabs and iOS 26 bottom-accessory support, `expo-glass-effect`, Victory Native, the health and widget libraries) were not researched in this round and need a version and maintenance check. The VoiceOver and TalkBack behaviour of Skia-drawn charts must be built and tested by hand. No controlled study shows that neutral over-target states or hide-numbers modes reduce harm. No SA-specific colour-meaning or device-mix data was found. And **none of the palettes has been seen by a user**. The ΔE figures model full dichromacy on a calibrated screen, not anomalous trichromats on a mid-range LCD in midday sun.

A two-week plan closes the riskiest gaps cheaply. **Days 1–3**: run a token audit in code (the CI contrast and ΔE script) and an **outdoor sunlight test** on three mid-range Android phones common in SA (one LCD, two OLED) at 50% and 100% brightness. Check whether carbs `#EA580C` bars and the scale dots stay readable, and thicken strokes if they do not. **Days 4–8**: hold remote moderated sessions with **8–10 SA adults**, including at least three with self-reported colour-vision deficiency, which you can recruit through screening questions. Show Today and the week chart in all three palettes in random order. Tasks: name the macro furthest from target with labels hidden and then shown, find over-target days, and rate how judged each palette feels on a 1–5 scale. Then do a first-click and tree test of the four-tab structure, and a timed FLSI-style tap audit of five logging tasks against MacroFactor and MFP. **Days 9–12**: review the check-in, coach and Calm-mode flows with **two or three people with lived experience of disordered eating and one dietitian**, ideally approached through SADAG or an SA ED clinician. Probe specifically for any screen, word or colour that reads as judgement. **Days 13–14**: test VoiceOver and TalkBack on device for the macro bars and trend chart, and check Dynamic Type at AX5. Success criteria: ≥90% correct macro identification with labels, no participant describing an over-target state as "failure", median logging taps at or below the FLSI targets, and every chart operable by screen reader.

## Conclusion

The palette question turns out to be a structure question. Once brand, data and status are separated into tokens that never cross, most choices follow from the constraints. The brand can be dark and calm because it no longer has to double as a nutrient. Calories can be ink because they are a budget, not a grade. Red returns to its one legitimate meaning, that something broke. That separation is also what makes Calm mode cheap to build: if colour never carried judgement, hiding the numbers leaves nothing evaluative behind. The stack follows the developer's call for UI headroom: React Native with Expo carries the interface, a TypeScript port of the engine keeps it working offline, and golden test vectors keep that port honest against the authoritative C# engine on the server. Native code is spent only where users touch the platform directly: widgets, screen readers and the glass chrome. The open risk is empirical. The tokens are mathematically sound, but whether SA users in bright sun read emerald fibre, orange carbs and an ink "met" bar the way the simulations predict is a two-week test, not a debate.
